# What is DevOps?

DevOps is a culture and set of practices that help development and operations teams collaborate to deliver software more quickly and reliably. It emphasizes automation, continuous feedback, quality, testing, monitoring, and shared responsibility throughout the software delivery lifecycle.

Organizations use DevOps practices to deliver new features and updates in shorter cycles while maintaining the quality and reliability of their applications.

> DevOps improves application delivery by combining collaboration and automation with continuous testing, quality practices, and monitoring.

## Why DevOps?

A simplified goal is to deliver value from the **developer to the customer** with a fast, reliable feedback loop.

In a traditional workflow, work might pass through separate groups: a system administrator provisions servers, an application or development team builds the software, testers verify it, and build or release engineers prepare and deploy it. Hand-offs between separate teams can take time and make communication and feedback difficult.

DevOps evolved to address these challenges. Development and operations work more closely together, share responsibility, and use automation to streamline building, testing, releasing, and operating software. DevOps does **not** necessarily mean that all roles are combined into one team; it means improving collaboration across the delivery process.

## How to introduce yourself for a DevOps position

Explain your background clearly and take the time you need to give a truthful, relevant answer. Describe what you have actually done, the tools and practices you have used, and the results or lessons from your work. Do not exaggerate your experience; for example, DevOps as a modern discipline has existed for roughly a decade and a half, so avoid claiming decades of DevOps experience if that does not reflect your background.

You can organize your introduction around these important DevOps practices:

- **Automation:** What tasks or infrastructure have you automated?
- **Quality:** How have you helped improve reliability or maintain standards?
- **Testing:** What testing or CI/CD workflows have you worked with?
- **Monitoring:** How have you monitored systems and responded to issues?

**Example:**

“Hi, I’m [name]. I have [length and type of relevant experience] in [your background or area]. I’ve worked with [tools and technologies] on [brief project or responsibility]. My experience includes [relevant examples of automation, quality, testing, and monitoring]. I’m interested in this role because [specific reason].”

Adapt the example to your own experience and be prepared to explain the projects and skills you mention.

## What are your day-to-day activities as a DevOps engineer?

Day-to-day responsibilities vary by organization, but may include:

- Maintaining and improving build, test, and deployment pipelines.
- Automating infrastructure provisioning and repetitive operational tasks.
- Managing cloud resources, servers, containers, and deployment environments.
- Monitoring application health, performance, and availability.
- Troubleshooting deployment problems and production incidents with development and operations teams.
- Improving the security, reliability, and observability of systems and delivery workflows.
- Reviewing changes, documenting processes, and helping teams release software safely.
- Using feedback and lessons from incidents to continuously improve systems and practices.
